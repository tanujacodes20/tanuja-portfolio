02 / Proof of work
Life

Nobody is watching. That's the good news.

On posting the thing you're scared to post.

+

I deleted my first post 11 minutes after publishing it.

It had three likes. One was my mom. I was convinced the whole internet had read it and quietly decided I wasn't worth following.

Here's what I learned the hard way: nobody is watching as closely as you think. People are busy worrying about their own posts, their own deadlines, their own three likes.

That sounds sad. It's actually freedom. If nobody is watching, you get to practice in public. You get to be bad, then less bad, then good, while the world scrolls past.

So post the thing. The worst case is silence, and silence is a pretty generous teacher.

Work

Your calendar is a confession.

What you say matters vs. where your hours actually go.

+

Tell me your priorities and I'll nod. Show me your calendar and I'll know.

Most of us say "deep work matters" and then book six meetings that could have been one message. We say "customers first" and then spend Friday formatting a deck no customer will see.

I started a small habit: every Sunday I color my week. Black for work that moves something forward. Grey for everything else.

The first week was almost entirely grey. Not because I was lazy, but because I was busy in all the wrong places.

Busy is easy. Useful is a choice you make in 30-minute blocks.

AI

AI can write. It can't care.

Why the best content in 2026 still has a human fingerprint.

+

Everyone can generate a post in four seconds now. That's exactly why most posts feel the same.

AI is a brilliant first draft machine. It's fast, tireless, and never stares at a blank page. I use it every day.

But it doesn't know that your founder almost quit in year two. It doesn't know the joke your team still tells about the launch that broke everything. It doesn't know what you'd never say.

The edge isn't the tool. It's taste: knowing which sentence to cut, which story to tell, which opinion is worth the risk.

Let the machine do the typing. Keep the caring for yourself.

GTM

Distribution is the product nobody ships.

Great products die quietly. Here's why.

+

The best product I ever saw had 40 users. The worst one I ever saw had 40,000.

The difference wasn't code. It was that one team treated distribution like an afterthought, and the other treated it like a feature.

Go-to-market isn't "post on launch day and pray." It's a system: who you're talking to, where they already hang out, what they'd forward to a friend, and how you'll know it's working.

Engineers build the product. GTM engineers build the road it travels on.

Build both, or build something nobody hears about.
